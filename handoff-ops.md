# /handoff-ops — status brief for ops and leadership

Goal: a one-page, skimmable summary of the real-world project or work-in-progress (the actual thing being built or run, not the Claude Project) that operations and leadership can read in a minute and discuss. Lead with tables; cut the fluff.

## Gather first

Pull from the conversation or Project: what the work is, where it stands, decisions made, what is stuck, and what comes next. If the picture is thin, ask the user for the missing pieces before writing.

## Output template

Use this structure. Keep it to roughly one page.

```
# [Project / initiative] — status
[Date] · Owner: [name] · For: Ops + Leadership

## TL;DR
2–3 lines: what this is, where it stands, what is needed from the reader.

## What this is
2–3 sentences on the goal and why it matters to the business.

## Status at a glance
| Workstream | Status | Owner | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ... | On track / At risk / Blocked | ... | ... |

## Decisions made
- [decision] — [why]
- ...

## Blockers, risks, open questions
| Item | Impact | What it needs | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ... | ... | ... | ... |

## What's next
| Next step | Target | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ... | ... | ... |

## For leadership
1–3 decisions or questions that need their input.

## Resources
Links and files for the discussion.
```

Use a plain status word or a 🟢 / 🟡 / 🔴 dot — whichever reads faster for the audience, consistently.

## Deliver it

Save it as a file the user can forward (a doc or markdown file), or render it as an artifact. Offer a polished, Sell to Sean–branded version (HTML or PDF) if it is going to leadership in a meeting. Show the draft for review before it is sent.
